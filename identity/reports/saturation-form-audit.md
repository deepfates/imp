# Identity Form Saturation Audit

## Scope And Method

This is an adversarial form audit, not a naming decision. It does not rank,
shortlist, reject, delete, availability-check, or select any observation.
It tests whether the corpus has actually explored the available lexical and
identity-system mechanisms for a pre-release Elixir/OTP library that declares,
calls, evaluates, optimizes, and operates LM programs, with a credible
artifact-optimization horizon. It preserves the product boundary: this is not
an official DSPy port, a static-proof system, a universal agent OS, a provider
SDK, or a guarantee of autonomous improvement.

Snapshot observed on 2026-07-13 after `run-16-negative-space-futures` arrived.
I read `README.md`, `docs/DSEX_PHILOSOPHY.md`,
`docs/RESEARCH_LANDSCAPE.md`, `identity/README.md`, and `identity/atlas.json`;
parsed every candidate's surface, rationale, etymology, pronunciation, grammar,
strategies, audience and architecture lenses, concerns, and wildcard state;
and inspected current code nomenclature in `mix.exs`, `lib/dsex.ex`,
`lib/dsex/telemetry.ex`, and `lib/mix/tasks/`. The current implementation has
the coupled `DSEx` / `:dsex` / `DSEx.Application` / `mix dsex.*` /
`[:dsex, ...]` system, not merely a package spelling.

## Missing Or Shallow Mechanisms

These are not merely low counts. A mechanism is called missing only when the
raw candidates do not exercise it; shallow means it appears incidentally or
only as an intentionally hostile wildcard.

- **Genuinely missing:** deliberate reduplication; deliberate rhyme; systematic
  alliteration; onomatopoeia; and ideophones. `Plans, Paths, and Programs` is
  the only explicit alliteration rationale. `Ghrm` mentions possible
  onomatopoeia only as an unpronounceable counterexample. None forms a
  controlled family, and no rationale identifies reduplication.
- **Genuinely missing:** backronyms. The corpus has constructed acronym
  expansions, but no evidence that an existing memorable surface was given a
  later, deliberately contestable expansion as a backronym mechanism.
- **Shallow:** clipping, elision, and abbreviative morphology. `Thaum`, `Ecor`,
  `Tranloss`, `Howsit`, `Decl`, `Sem`, and `IR-ish` exist, but no run compares
  clipped roots against their recoverability, expansion, and code grammar.
- **Shallow:** true blends versus simple compounds and suffixing. The shared
  `compound-portmanteau` strategy count obscures whether a form actually fuses
  two recoverable stems. `Procotol` also demonstrates the danger: it is likely
  heard as a typo rather than a blend.
- **Shallow:** grammatical conversion. The corpus uses noun/verb ambiguity,
  but 1,393 observations omit `grammar`; claims that a form works as command,
  library, event, and institution are mostly untested.
- **Shallow:** wordlike initialisms and dual readings. `ORUM` and `MORA` name
  the ambiguity, but no system tests which pronunciation is remembered,
  whether the expansion is recoverable, or when the ordinary-word reading
  should dominate.
- **Shallow:** numerals, punctuation, and namespace forms. They are well
  represented as raw counterexamples, but 611 space-containing and 181
  punctuation-containing surfaces cannot directly be the current Hex package,
  OTP atom, module root, Mix prefix, config prefix, and telemetry prefix. This
  is not a rejection of those observations; it shows that their identity-layer
  role has not been specified.
- **Shallow:** eponyms. The proper/mythic/historical tag is now 75 observations,
  but it merges researchers, gods, venues, culturally specific figures, and
  historical objects. Citation tone, attribution, cultural permission, and
  future company-versus-library use have not been independently exercised.
- **Shallow:** cross-language forms. Run 11 appropriately records uncertainty
  and concern for every borrowing, but supplies no pronunciations or
  architecture lenses. Its transliterations and source-relay coinages must not
  count as linguistically reviewed forms until source-language reviewers assess
  meaning, grammar, transliteration, and social context.
- **Shallow:** anti-brands. They are unusually well represented, especially in
  runs 09, 13, 15, and 16, but mostly function as slogans or negative tests.
  The missing question is how a bounded counter-claim coexists with a clear
  project, company, community, and product-family grammar.

## Code, Speech, And Architecture Blind Spots

The portfolio schema records optional pronunciation, grammar, and rationale;
the enrichment schema correctly requires spoken forms, six code forms, four
prose forms, architecture forms, international notes, and future-scope notes.
No `registry.jsonl`, enrichment events, assessments, flags, dissent, or prior
reports are present in the observed `identity/` tree. Therefore none of those
required embodiments has actually been produced.

- **Speech, dictation, spelling, and accessibility:** 1,507 observations lack
  pronunciation, and no raw record contains a spoken recommendation or support
  call. `Kzrt`, `Aeua`, `Qoa`, `Xae`, `Ghrm`, `R/0`, and `?=>!` correctly expose
  failure modes, but they do not test successful dictation or screen-reader
  output. The 193 pronunciation-bearing records are concentrated in runs 04,
  10, 13, and 15.
- **Search and documentation:** no searchability study, query corpus, README
  headline, paper title, conference sentence, error sentence, or citation form
  exists for any raw candidate. This audit performed no external availability
  or trademark search. Searchability here means unassisted retrieval of the
  intended project and written recovery from speech, not domain clearance.
- **Code nomenclature:** run 12 names the real surfaces in rationales, e.g.
  `Program.Mesh` admits that a dot helps an Elixir alias but hurts Hex and
  speech; `CNP` claims a log/config/telemetry role while acknowledging opacity;
  `beam.run/1` admits that arity syntax fails nearly every non-Elixir surface.
  Those are useful concerns, not executable projections. There is no recorded
  `hex_package`, `otp_app`, `module_root`, `mix_task_prefix`, `config_prefix`,
  or `telemetry_prefix` for any observation.
- **Architecture:** all atlas architecture IDs are represented, but 200
  observations have no architecture lens at all (the whole civic and
  cross-cultural portfolios), and `oss-commercial-split` occurs only 19 times.
  Run 15 adds meaningful role grammar such as master, research program,
  community, descriptor, and code root, yet it remains candidate narrative,
  not a cross-role system test.
- **Audience lenses:** every atlas audience is intended by at least one run,
  but `elixir-newcomers` appears in only one intended run and
  `security-legal-procurement` has only 32 candidate lenses. The civic run
  supplies no candidate-level audience or architecture lenses, which makes its
  claimed audience breadth untestable.
- **Future identities:** the research landscape explicitly expands the artifact
  horizon to instructions, demonstrations, tool descriptions, retrieval
  policies, playbooks, schemas, code fragments, and configuration. The corpus
  repeatedly names program, runtime, optimizer, evidence, and trace, but lacks
  a tested grammar for how one master identity extends truthfully across a
  library, research project, protocol, community, hosted product, company, and
  future artifact planes without making an unsupported platform claim.

Anti-convergence rules for this wave:

- Generators see this matrix and the product boundary, but no existing surfaces,
  leaders, scores, or candidate lists.
- Do not reuse a previous surface, a simple case/hyphen/space variant, or its
  first two content stems. Record any accidental overlap rather than repairing
  it.
- At least two different generators or methods own nonadjacent cells; no model
  owns more than three cells. Preserve all malformed or hostile observations.
- Do not use `-ex`, `-ly`, `-io`, `AI`, `LM`, `bot`, or a Latinate ending as a
  default escape hatch. They remain allowed only when the rationale states why
  that morphology is necessary.
- Every observation records pronunciation, grammar, etymology or an explicit
  `coined/no source` statement, audience lenses, architecture lenses, and the
  six code-form projections or a precise reason it is intentionally non-code.
- Keep exactly 20 wildcards, distributed across cells rather than concentrated
  in sound or punctuation forms.

## Falsifiable Saturation Tests

Saturation is not established by the present counts. It is established only
when all of the following tests pass without deleting difficult observations.

1. **Independent lineage test:** no two purportedly independent runs have the
   same ordered candidate payload after provenance fields are removed. The
   current `run-01` / `run-01b` pair fails this test.
2. **Mechanism matrix test:** manually tag every observation with compound,
   blend, clipping, affixation, conversion, sound figure, reduplication,
   ideophone, acronym class, code token, dual reading, counter-claim, eponym
   class, and source-language status. Each required mechanism must have at
   least 10 non-wildcard observations from two independent runs and two
   territories; zero means genuinely missing, not merely under-counted.
3. **Challenge-wave stability test:** run two independent 100-observation
   challenge waves after the matrix exists. Each may add at most 5% new
   mechanism/territory cells, matching the atlas criterion, and neither may
   expose an unrepresented audience or architecture role.
4. **Embodiment test:** create one valid enrichment event for every raw
   occurrence. It must contain a recommendation, support-call phrase, the six
   code forms, four prose forms, an architecture form, international notes, and
   future-scope notes. The current corpus fails at zero enrichment events.
5. **Speech and accessibility test:** for every non-deliberately-nonverbal
   form, three independent listeners write the intended surface from a spoken
   recommendation and identify its pronunciation. Require at least 80% exact
   recovery overall and record screen-reader speech for code-token forms; do
   not hide failures by normalizing them away.
6. **Code-system test:** mechanically validate each projected Hex package, OTP
   app atom, Elixir alias root, Mix task prefix, config prefix, and telemetry
   prefix against a generated fixture. It must compile and coexist as a family;
   a punctuation or phrase observation may pass only by explicitly occupying a
   non-code role with a linked implementation identity.
7. **Documentation, citation, and retrieval test:** independently generate a
   README headline, paper citation sentence, conference introduction, error
   line, and an unassisted local-project search query for each candidate. Test
   that the form is recoverable and that no sentence adds proof, safety,
   official-status, or platform claims. This is not an availability check.
8. **Architecture truth test:** for each candidate-role pairing, state whether
   it can truthfully name an OSS library, research effort, community, service,
   company, protocol, and product family. A role must be marked unavailable
   when the form implies authority or scope the product does not have; no form
   is required to cover every role.
9. **Cross-language stewardship test:** a competent reviewer for the named
   source language confirms or rejects each meaning, morphology,
   transliteration, pronunciation, and contextual use. Unreviewed forms stay
   in the corpus but do not count toward cross-language saturation.

Until these tests pass, the corpus is broad and useful but not saturated. This
conclusion is about missing evidence and form mechanisms, not the merit of any
candidate.
