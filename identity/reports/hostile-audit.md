# Hostile Identity Audit

This audit attacks the checkpoint's strongest conclusions rather than adding
another score. It covers the complete 2,052-entity corpus, all 6,156 canonical
model assessments, the international screen, collision records, reliability
report, and deterministic review pools. It does not select a name.

## Verdict

The corpus is broad enough and reproducible enough to support the owner's blind
candidate comparison. It is not evidence that a final identity is culturally,
legally, linguistically, or accessibly cleared. Scenario ranks are product-
context sensitivity projections over one global model-assessment matrix, not
audience research and not proof that one candidate fits every brand role.

## Findings

### The jury is global, not audience-specific

All 6,156 assessment records have an empty context object. The six scenarios
reweight the same 18-axis vectors; they do not contain separate judgments from
maintainers, newcomers, contributors, buyers, affected publics, or disabled
accessibility practitioners. Those audiences are represented in the atlas and
generation corpus, but their reactions remain hypotheses until people in those
roles participate.

### Agreement is useful but scale-biased

Scenario pairwise Spearman correlations range from 0.692 to 0.743 and
consistency ICC for the three-profile mean ranges from 0.891 to 0.903. Absolute-
agreement ICC for a single profile is only 0.456 to 0.490 because Flash scores
systematically higher than Sonnet and Terra. Equal-profile means are therefore
appropriate for navigation, but raw score distances should not be treated as
calibrated measurements. Lineage independence, BEAM resonance, and
international behavior are the weakest absolute-agreement axes.

### One technical metaphor can impersonate a product theory

The score frontier concentrates runtime and technical compounds such as
ScopeTree, Processloom, Faultcell, and Faultglass. Those candidates may be good
subsystem names while still being too narrow for a company, community,
protocol, or research field. Conversely, Draftifact, Evidence Loop, and Pinned
Lineage read naturally as category or method descriptors; scenario rank alone
does not make them umbrella brands.

### Code projections can erase the intended form

Identifier validity is not the same as identity preservation. `ScopeTree`
normally becomes `Scopetree`, `GenLine` becomes `Genline`, `beam.run` becomes
`beam_run` or `BeamRun`, and `Run.Pack` becomes `run_pack` or `RunPack`. A final
candidate needs a tested package, OTP application, module, Mix task, config,
telemetry, repository, and spoken contract, not merely a valid projection.

### Machine international evidence is an attention mechanism

Every one of the 2,052 enrichments has at least one unverified international
dimension and zero have human cultural, native-speaker, accessibility-user, or
legal validation. Model reasoning that sounds confident about pronunciation or
international behavior does not upgrade an unknown into observed evidence.

### Pure ranks are diagnostics, not narrowing sets

The raw top-25 score previews contain four wildcards each, or 16 percent. They
remain visible because altering them would hide the ranking output. The separate
scenario-deliberation pools deterministically replace the lowest-ranked
non-wildcards with the next ranked wildcards until the declared 20 percent floor
is met. The complete 439-entity wildcard pool also remains recoverable.

### Collision facts do not settle identity risk

The 1,682 active package-collision flags cover 667 candidates across Hex, npm,
PyPI, and crates.io. They are factual registry observations, not trademark or
company clearance. Institutional authority, technical implication, cultural
meaning, pronunciation, tone, and accessibility concerns belong in dissent and
human gates rather than being mislabeled as collision facts.

## Corrections Applied

- Replaced cross-provider confidence weighting with an equal mean of the three
  independent profile scores while retaining confidence as metadata.
- Consolidated factual flags at `identity/flags.jsonl` and corrected the event
  versus unique-candidate counts.
- Added frontier-specific model disagreement so global disagreement sampling
  cannot miss every current scenario leader.
- Added scenario-deliberation pools that enforce the declared wildcard floor
  without mutating the raw score previews.
- Recorded candidate-level reinterpretations, holds, and resurrections in
  `identity/dissent.jsonl`; none silently removes a candidate.

## Required Human Gates

1. Enter the owner's unrevealed candidate through the same registry,
   enrichment, collision, assessment, and projection protocol before discussing
   whether it was the expected answer.
2. Compare candidates within explicit roles: master brand, product or library,
   category descriptor, community or research effort, protocol, and subsystem.
3. Run blinded speech trials with at least three independent listeners,
   screen-reader and dictation checks, and at least 80 percent exact written
   recovery across trials before accepting pronunciation and spelling.
4. Run connotation sessions with maintainers, newcomers, contributors,
   buyers or procurement stakeholders, affected publics, and disabled
   accessibility practitioners using the actual product description.
5. Obtain qualified language and community review for cross-language, proper-
   name, or culturally anchored finalists, including Solfa and Telic Beam if
   they remain active.
6. Manually clear repositories, companies, products, package namespaces,
   relevant marks, and intended jurisdictions. Registry absence is not legal
   clearance.

## Completion Meaning

Step 8 fixes the search corpus and the decision protocol before the hidden
candidate appears. Completion means the owner can inspect the complete frontier,
its provenance, model agreement, factual flags, dissent, and unresolved human
gates. It does not mean a winner has been selected or that the eventual rename
is already authorized.
